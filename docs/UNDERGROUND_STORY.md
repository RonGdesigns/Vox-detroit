# Underground Career Story Framework

The underground route is a full story path, not a side minigame.

## Core theme

The player begins with ordinary economic pressure. Underground opportunities appear through people and circumstances rather than through a glowing “become criminal” menu option.

The player can:
- decline completely,
- participate occasionally,
- specialize,
- build deeper contacts,
- leave the route,
- attempt to convert money/connections into legitimate opportunities,
- or escalate until the underground story becomes one of the dominant campaign threads.

## Cannabis split

### Licensed route

The legal cannabis route is a regulated retail/business career:
- employment,
- inventory/compliance,
- customer service,
- management,
- legal ownership path.

### Unlicensed route

The underground cannabis route has:
- lower initial barriers,
- greater legal exposure,
- informal relationships,
- unstable trust,
- potential conflict with licensed business opportunities.

The game should recognize that these two worlds coexist without treating them as mechanically identical.

## Other substance categories

Use broad categories with deliberately different risk profiles.

### Stimulants

Game emphasis:
- higher legal exposure,
- volatile customer/contact behavior,
- increased personal-safety risk,
- stronger underground-network requirements.

### Opioids / pills

Game emphasis:
- very high customer-health consequences,
- dependency-related story events,
- moral/relationship consequences,
- heightened law-enforcement and community pressure.

### Psychedelics

Game emphasis:
- different customer base,
- lower repeat-demand assumptions than dependency-driven categories,
- different social reputation and legal risk profile.

### Sedatives

Game emphasis:
- diversion/theft story hooks,
- customer-health risk,
- unreliable contacts.

### Synthetics

Game emphasis:
- unpredictable quality/risk,
- volatile supply,
- very high consequence events.

These are narrative/gameplay categories, not real-world operating guides.

## Core underground stats

### Trust

How much contacts believe the player is reliable.

Unlocks:
- introductions,
- information,
- story access,
- larger opportunities.

### Heat

A general exposure meter.

Heat rises from story events, mistakes and escalating activity. It is not based on real-world police-evasion tactics.

High heat can cause:
- contacts disappearing,
- surveillance-themed story events,
- increased police encounters,
- legitimate employers reacting,
- family/friend concern,
- business opportunities closing.

### Street reputation

Separate from trust.

A feared player, a reliable player and a well-liked player should not all be represented by one number.

### Personal safety

Some paths create greater exposure to robbery, betrayal or violent characters.

### Community impact

The game can remember consequences in neighborhoods and relationships.

## Narrative structure

### Stage 1 — Opportunity

The player is introduced to an underground contact while money is tight.

The first choice should feel tempting but optional.

### Stage 2 — Connections

Success exposes multiple contacts with conflicting personalities and motives.

The player begins choosing who to trust.

### Stage 3 — Specialization

Different substance categories and criminal relationships become available.

The player does not need to participate in all of them.

### Stage 4 — Conflict

Consequences emerge:
- debt,
- betrayal,
- a contact in trouble,
- police attention,
- legitimate employer/business conflict,
- harmed relationship,
- neighborhood reaction.

### Stage 5 — Direction

The player can:
- retreat,
- cooperate with a legal opportunity,
- remain small,
- become more deeply involved,
- use legitimate businesses as a new life direction,
- or pursue a high-risk criminal ending.

## Story principle

The underground path should sometimes be financially attractive precisely because its consequences are qualitatively different from normal work.

If it is simply “higher pay + a wanted meter,” the route will feel shallow.
