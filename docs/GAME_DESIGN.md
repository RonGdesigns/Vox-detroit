# Vox Detroit — Game Design Foundation

## High concept

A standalone voxel open-world life RPG in a geographically recognizable Detroit. The player begins with limited money, housing and connections, then builds a life through work, relationships, property, business and story choices. The city is not only scenery: it can physically change as properties are renovated, businesses open or close, construction happens and neighborhoods evolve.

## Design pillars

### 1. Detroit is gameplay
Travel distance, neighborhoods, roads, properties and landmarks matter. The city should be recognizable enough that local geography influences navigation and decisions.

### 2. Work should be played, not clicked
Jobs use interactive loops. Delivery means navigating and delivering. Construction means repairing/building. Mechanic work means diagnosing and replacing parts. Better skill and reputation unlock better work.

### 3. Money must have pressure and purpose
Income competes with rent, utilities, food, transportation, maintenance, business expenses and property investment. Wealth should expand options rather than merely increase a score.

### 4. The city remembers
NPC relationships, employers, neighborhoods and organizations react to player choices. Reputation is contextual rather than one universal morality meter.

### 5. The world can change physically
Voxel construction supports renovation, demolition and development. A vacant lot can become a construction site and later a finished building. Player-owned property can be visibly upgraded.

### 6. Story and simulation support each other
The main story is discovered through the life the player builds. Different jobs, contacts and neighborhoods expose different information and opportunities.

## Starting player fantasy

The player begins with:
- a modest amount of cash,
- basic housing,
- no established career,
- limited transportation,
- a phone/contact system,
- a small social network.

The early game is about stability. The middle game is about specialization, relationships and ownership. The late game is about influence: companies, properties, crews, neighborhood change and major story consequences.

## Initial job families

- Delivery / courier
- Construction / renovation
- Mechanic / auto service
- Restaurant / food service
- Photography / media
- Security
- Rideshare / taxi
- Property renovation / management

Each job family should support progression from worker → specialist/manager → owner/operator when it makes sense.

## Economy layers

1. Cash and bank balance
2. Wages and tips
3. Recurring expenses
4. Transportation ownership and upkeep
5. Housing and rent
6. Property ownership
7. Business ownership
8. Credit/loans later
9. Investment and passive income later

## NPC simulation target

Important NPCs can have:
- home,
- workplace,
- schedule,
- relationships,
- money state,
- vehicle/transit preference,
- memories/flags,
- reputation toward the player,
- story state.

Crowd/background NPCs can use a lighter simulation tier.

## Narrative structure

The main story should not require a strict mission ladder. Story threads are exposed through jobs, neighborhoods, contacts and property/business activity. Some information can be discovered from multiple routes so the player is not punished for choosing a different career.

## First playable slice

The first playable slice is deliberately small:
- one recognizable Downtown Detroit test district,
- one apartment,
- one enterable business,
- one job loop,
- wallet/bank,
- a few scheduled NPCs,
- one vehicle prototype,
- one short branching story interaction.

The goal is to prove that the city, economy and story reinforce one another before expanding the map.
