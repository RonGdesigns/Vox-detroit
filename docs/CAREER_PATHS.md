# Career and Income Paths

Vox Detroit should let the player build a life through many different forms of income rather than force a single career ladder.

The player may combine paths. A character can work a normal job, drive for hire at night, own a small business later, or enter an underground storyline. Career choices affect money, reputation, relationships, access, story events and risk.

## Career families

### Wage employment

Examples:
- mail carrier,
- parcel-delivery driver,
- restaurant/fast-food crew,
- warehouse worker,
- factory/production worker,
- hotel employee,
- security officer,
- janitorial/grounds worker,
- healthcare support worker,
- office/admin worker,
- retail worker,
- transit operator.

### Gig work

Examples:
- taxi/rideshare,
- delivery courier,
- freelance photography/video,
- handyman,
- freelance computer repair,
- short contract labor.

Gig work offers flexibility but less stable income.

### Skilled trades

Examples:
- mechanic,
- electrician,
- construction worker,
- HVAC technician,
- carpenter,
- building maintenance,
- vehicle/body repair.

These paths should connect strongly to the voxel world because players can physically repair or construct things.

### Professional careers

Examples:
- software developer,
- engineer,
- accountant,
- designer,
- photographer/media professional,
- healthcare professional,
- property manager.

Professional jobs should not become “wait eight hours and get paid.” They need task gameplay, deadlines, client/employer relationships and advancement.

### Business ownership

A career can eventually become ownership:
- restaurant,
- repair shop,
- contractor,
- delivery company,
- media studio,
- property company,
- retail shop,
- licensed cannabis business.

Ownership shifts gameplay from completing every task personally toward staffing, purchasing, scheduling, quality, finances and strategic decisions.

### Regulated cannabis

Michigan's legal adult-use system gives this path a different fantasy from the underground market.

Possible progression:
- retail employee,
- shift lead,
- inventory/compliance role,
- manager,
- licensed-business ownership path later.

Gameplay can involve customer service, queue management, regulated inventory, store security, product knowledge, stock discrepancies and inspections.

The legal cannabis path should feel like a regulated retail/business career, not an illegal-dealing reskin.

### Underground economy

The underground route is a separate narrative/career ecosystem.

It can include:
- cannabis sold outside the licensed market,
- illicit stimulants,
- opioids/pills,
- psychedelics,
- sedatives,
- synthetic substances.

The game should model these as abstract market categories. It does not need real-world recipes, sourcing instructions, concealment methods or trafficking procedures.

Each category has a different combination of:
- legal exposure,
- violence exposure,
- customer-health consequences,
- dependency/customer instability,
- supply volatility,
- neighborhood reputation,
- police attention/heat,
- trust with contacts.

The important design principle is that “illegal drug dealing” is not one universal risk meter.

## Parallel progression

The player should never be permanently locked into one life path.

Examples:

- mail carrier by day + photographer on weekends,
- warehouse worker → mechanic training → repair shop owner,
- restaurant worker → manager → restaurant owner,
- programmer → freelance contractor → software studio,
- dispensary employee → manager → legal cannabis business,
- legal job → underground contacts → criminal route,
- underground route → attempt to leave it and transition into legitimate business.

Story characters should react to the player's history, not only the currently selected job.

## Career consequences

Every path can modify:
- income stability,
- available contacts,
- neighborhood reputation,
- credit/financial access,
- story opportunities,
- schedule/time pressure,
- personal safety,
- police/legal attention,
- relationship reactions,
- future ownership opportunities.

No career should exist solely because it pays a different hourly wage.
