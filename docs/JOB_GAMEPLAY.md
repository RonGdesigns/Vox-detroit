# Job Gameplay Design

A job is only worth adding if its moment-to-moment gameplay can be fun.

The design target is:

**job fantasy + skill + decisions + complications + progression**

not:

**clock in → wait → receive money**

## Universal job structure

Most jobs should contain five layers:

1. **Preparation** — choose route, tools, loadout, schedule or priorities.
2. **Execution** — perform the physical/mental task.
3. **Complications** — traffic, weather, broken equipment, customers, mistakes, emergencies or changing requirements.
4. **Evaluation** — speed, accuracy, safety, quality, customer satisfaction or waste.
5. **Progression** — harder assignments, better pay, new equipment, authority, ownership.

## Mail carrier

Core loop:
- sort a route,
- load mail/packages,
- travel a walking/driving route,
- identify correct addresses,
- handle packages requiring special interaction,
- return undeliverable items.

Gameplay pressure:
- route efficiency,
- weather,
- apartment access,
- incorrect addresses,
- package volume,
- time windows.

Progression:
- larger routes,
- vehicle routes,
- priority deliveries,
- route supervisor.

## Parcel delivery driver

Core loop:
- load a van as a spatial/priority puzzle,
- choose route order,
- drive through traffic,
- locate entrances,
- carry packages,
- handle signatures/business deliveries.

Complications:
- road closures,
- oversized package,
- apartment buzzer problems,
- customer not available,
- fragile package,
- vehicle problem.

A good player becomes faster because they learn the city and organize intelligently, not because a stat simply increases.

## Taxi / rideshare

Core loop:
- accept/select trip,
- navigate to pickup,
- identify passenger,
- drive safely/quickly,
- react to passenger requests,
- complete drop-off.

Complications:
- events causing traffic,
- impatient passengers,
- wrong pickup pin,
- multiple stops,
- passenger conversations that reveal side stories,
- vehicle cleanliness/fuel/maintenance.

Progression:
- better vehicle,
- airport/event trips,
- premium clientele,
- private-driver work.

## Software developer

Avoid asking the player to literally type real code.

Use game-like abstractions:
- connect logic nodes,
- diagnose a bug from symptoms,
- choose implementation tradeoffs,
- repair broken flows,
- optimize a system under a performance budget,
- respond to changing client requirements,
- deploy before a deadline.

Complications:
- vague requirements,
- regressions,
- production incident,
- difficult client,
- team conflict,
- technical debt.

Progression:
junior → developer → senior/lead → freelancer → software company.

## Fast-food / quick-service restaurant

Use a fictional chain.

Core loop:
- take orders,
- assemble food,
- manage cooking timers,
- coordinate stations,
- handle drive-through and counter queues,
- restock and clean.

The fun comes from controlled chaos: several systems demanding attention simultaneously.

Progression:
crew → station specialist → shift lead → manager → franchise/business ownership.

## Restaurant

A sit-down restaurant can emphasize:
- table flow,
- kitchen timing,
- ingredients,
- customer satisfaction,
- reservations/events,
- staffing.

This should feel distinct from fast food.

## Warehouse / distribution center

Core loop:
- receive shipment,
- scan/locate items,
- pick orders,
- pack correctly,
- stage outgoing freight,
- operate equipment.

Complications:
- wrong inventory count,
- rush shipment,
- damaged item,
- blocked aisle,
- equipment failure.

Progression can lead into logistics coordination or warehouse management.

## Manufacturing / auto production

Detroit-specific flavor without using real company branding.

Possible tasks:
- station timing,
- component installation,
- quality inspection,
- diagnose a production defect,
- machine setup,
- maintenance response.

Progression:
line worker → skilled station → quality/maintenance → supervisor/technical role.

## Mechanic

One of the strongest fits for the project.

Loop:
- inspect symptoms,
- diagnose system,
- order/find correct part,
- remove/replace components,
- test vehicle.

Jobs can range from basic service to major repairs and restoration.

Eventually:
employee → independent mechanic → shop owner.

## Construction / renovation

The voxel world makes this especially powerful.

Loop:
- read job requirements,
- acquire materials,
- remove damaged pieces,
- build/repair physical structure,
- inspect quality.

A renovation actually changes the saved city.

## Healthcare support

Keep the gameplay around logistics/service rather than turning medical care into a simplistic arcade action.

Examples:
- transport patients/items,
- prepare rooms,
- restock supplies,
- coordinate requests,
- respond to priority calls.

## Security

Loop:
- patrol,
- monitor access,
- respond to alarms,
- talk to people,
- decide whether an event is harmless or escalating,
- document incidents.

Different sites can radically change the feel: office tower, event venue, apartment building, store.

## Licensed cannabis retail

Core loop:
- customer queue,
- ID/eligibility checks,
- inventory lookup,
- customer preference matching,
- register accuracy,
- regulated stock counts,
- security/compliance events.

Progression:
employee → lead → manager → ownership/business-management route.

## Underground dealer route

This route should be story-heavy and tense rather than a repetitive “sell item” button.

Gameplay focuses on abstract decisions:
- who the player trusts,
- which opportunities they accept,
- how much exposure they tolerate,
- relationships with customers/contacts,
- whether to expand or stay small,
- how criminal income affects legitimate relationships/businesses.

Different substance categories have different risk profiles. High-risk categories can create more severe health, violence and legal consequences even if the short-term profit is greater.

The route should create meaningful reasons to:
- refuse an opportunity,
- leave the business,
- cooperate with one character,
- betray another character,
- transition toward legal income,
- or escalate further.

## Detroit job roster priority

Recent Detroit-area employment data supports emphasizing:
- office/admin work,
- production/manufacturing,
- transportation/material moving,
- food service,
- sales/retail,
- healthcare,
- engineering,
- construction/repair,
- computer/technical work.

These should form the backbone of the everyday-job world rather than filling the city with only glamorous careers.
